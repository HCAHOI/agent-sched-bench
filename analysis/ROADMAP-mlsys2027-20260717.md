## Verdict + thesis

Direction survives only as ONE systems paper after surgery. As pitched
(4 pillars) it is a stats paper stapled to a systems paper; each half dies
under the respective expert reviewer.

**Thesis (one sentence):** an agent-serving scheduler that treats every
KV-cache action (swap / evict+recompute / keep) as a load-priced functional
of one per-workload certified survival curve, with an anytime-valid gate
deploying and revoking each fitted policy online — subsuming Continuum's
CDF-TTL and ThunderAgent's memoryless decay as the uncertified, unpriced
special cases; beats both where the data licenses it and is NEVER WORSE than
the prior-free fallback where it doesn't. "Never worse" is the load-bearing
claim; it is what findings 1-12 actually proved.

## Online-gate validity paragraph (seed text for W3, not a TODO)

Per-call paired regret between the deployed trigger and the deadline fallback
is computable from the observed tool duration alone, because both arms' costs
are deterministic functions of that duration — valid iff the action does not
perturb the tool's own runtime (exogeneity), which our contention probe
supports at the tool level but ThunderAgent Fig 7a contradicts for
serving-side latency; therefore the gate monitors tool-call-level regret and
serving-side effects are measured separately in the system eval. The
e-process bets are predictable (fixed before each call's duration resolves),
so deploy/revoke at arbitrary data-dependent times preserves the type-I
guarantee. Within-task correlation breaks call-level independence, so
increments are task-clustered — the same clustering unit as
`paired_task_cluster_bootstrap`. Policy adaptivity (which calls fire) does
not bias the pair, since both arms are scored on every call regardless of the
action taken.
