## Bottom line

The lead's hypothesis is **partly right, partly wrong.**

- **Right:** on a *per-task* basis, TB carries more heavy op mass. Expected
  swappable mass per task at the 3500 ms anchor is **103 s (TB) vs 56 s
  (SWE-rebench)** — nearly 2x — and TB's tail is heavier (86% of TB tool-time
  sits in calls over 10 s vs 72% for SWE). A single TB task tends to contain a
  bigger, longer build/compile/train op.
- **Wrong:** the *fraction* of calls that are heavy is essentially identical
  between the two corpora (6.3% vs 6.1% over 3500 ms; 4.8% vs 4.9% over 5000 ms).
  SWE-rebench is not "light on heavy calls" — it has proportionally the same
  heavy-call rate, it just also carries a huge mass of near-instant calls
  (P50 = 5 ms; lots of tiny reads/greps) that drag its per-call medians down.
- **Total swappable mass still favors SWE-rebench** (15.6 ks vs 8.6 ks at 3500)
  purely because it has 277 tasks vs 83. Per unit of *collection*, TB is the
  denser showcase; in absolute terms today, fresh-277 already holds more.

So TB is the better *per-task* showcase workload, and buying more TB does look
worthwhile if the goal is dense heavy-op tasks — but the headline "SWE is light"
framing conflates per-task mass (true) with heavy-call rate (false).

## What more TB would buy

Extrapolating from TB's current per-task heavy-call rate (rough, see caveats):
reproducing fresh-277's **845 heavy calls (>3500 ms)** would take roughly
**~390 TB tasks** — because TB averages ~2.2 heavy calls/task vs SWE's ~3.0.
Conversely, ~59 SWE tasks already match TB's current 180-heavy-call total. If the
metric is *swappable seconds* rather than *heavy-call count*, TB catches up much
faster (higher mass per heavy call), so the "N tasks" figure depends heavily on
whether you value count or mass.

## Caveats (read before acting)

1. **Model confound — the big one.** SWE-rebench fresh-277 was run with
   **qwen3.7-max**; the TB dev corpus was run with **GLM-5.2**. Every workload
   difference here conflates *benchmark* with *agent model*. GLM-5.2 may simply
   issue fewer, chunkier commands than qwen3.7-max regardless of benchmark. A
   clean TB-vs-SWE comparison would need the same model on both; this memo
   cannot separate the two effects.
2. **TB is dev-exposed.** This TB root (`zai-org-GLM-5.2/20260709T171830`) is one
   of the `excluded_trace_roots` in the fresh-277 certification manifest — it was
   used during method development. It is fine for *sizing* but must never be
   used as held-out evaluation data.
3. **Per-task mass divides by 83, not 100.** 17 of the 100 TB tasks issued zero
   `tool_exec` calls (read-only or failed-to-start), so TB per-task means are
   over the 83 active tasks. Over all 100 tasks the per-task mass would be ~17%
   lower.
4. **Tail is dominated by a few extreme calls.** Both corpora have max calls in
   the hundreds of seconds (SWE 346 s, TB 600 s). Per-task mass means are highly
   skewed; the P90-per-task figures (135 s SWE / 143 s TB at 3500) are much
   closer than the means and are the more robust comparison.
5. **Verb tokenizer artifacts.** The heavy-verb mix shows shell keywords
   (`then`, `done`) as "verbs" — these leak from `if/for/while` constructs where
   the last sequential segment starts with a keyword. This is a known
   granularity limit of `shell_command_segments` (escaped/loop separators), not a
   real verb class. It affects a handful of calls and does not change the mass
   totals; treat those rows as "compound shell construct".
6. **Extrapolation is a per-task-rate heuristic**, assuming future TB tasks are
   exchangeable with these 83. Real new TB tasks will vary; treat "~390 tasks"
   as an order-of-magnitude sizing number, not a procurement target.
